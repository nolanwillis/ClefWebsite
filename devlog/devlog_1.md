# Dev Log #1: Start

*Published on October 16, 2026 by **Nolan Willis***

## Welcome 

First I’d like to start with a bit of an introduction. My name is Nolan Willis, I’m a twenty-five year-old software engineer, who graduated with a Masters in Game Programming about sixteen months ago from DePaul University. Since graduating I’ve applied to over two-hundred jobs in both web and game development, four of which were phishing scams, and only one resulted in an interview. During this period of joblessness I’ve done game jams, built projects, learned new tech stacks, and reached out to several game studios and software startups. Despite this, and using my personal network of family and friends, I still have no job. After six years of school and almost a hundred thousand dollars in debt this is not where I thought I would be. 

The job market for entry level software engineers is virtually non-existent. Now you might be wondering why I’m staying in this field. Why I haven’t given up and pursued another line of work where working hard actually pays off. Well, to put it simply I can’t really do anything else. I was born with a rare, terminal, neuromuscular condition called Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy, that limits my work capabilities to a computer. It’s the most aggressive form of Muscular Dystrophy and usually kills you by your late twenties or early thirties. At the rate I’m declining, barring any breakthrough in duchenne treatment, I will be dead by thirty-five. 

## What would you do with ten years left? 

My bitter sweet, fuck you answer to this question, an answer I’ve heard more times I care to count, is you make the best of it. True, there are other computer based jobs, but the ones I’m qualified for don’t interest me in the slightest and not using the education I worked so hard to get would be a complete waste.            

As of writing, it is October 13th 2026, 3:24 pm. At 10:00 am this morning I was supposed to have a job interview with a small indie studio. After spending thirty hours last week making a demo, writing a one page summary of the demo, and diligently preparing for the interview, it was cancelled at 4 am this morning with no explanation. I expect this level of unprofessionalism from larger studios, but not from smaller ones that I applied to with an open application email, which clearly depicted my skills via a resume and thoughtful letter.

So, in light of this experience and the past year of no results, I’m giving up on applying to jobs. Instead of wasting my remaining time trying to prove to others that I can program, I’m going to achieve the following two goals: finish The Partition, which is a book I’ve been writing for the last two years, and release Clef.  

## What is Clef? 

In January I wrote a twenty page game design doc outlining a music themed metroidvania, called Clef. The idea was to make a dark, gritty, story driven metroidvania-lite that takes place across the six realms of the magical musical realm of Clavis. The player controls a Conductor — a warrior musician sworn to protect the realm.  In this game each realm would be a genre of music with themed abilities, enemies and allies. Along with the game design doc, I implemented a somewhat advanced 2D movement controller in Godot. 

## Moving forward

The main issue with solo projects of substantial scale is that I usually start with a lot of energy, investing every chance I get into working on it, just to inevitably burn out a month later and move on. So to combat this I’m going to do the following: 

* Make a white boxed MVP first, which should be finished in six months on April 13th 2027. 
* Every work week, have clearly defined objectives (a sprint if you will) with a time investment of  ~20 hrs / week. 
* No working on the weekends. 
* Write a weekly dev log and upload it to the website.
* Maintain a list of finished objectives for motivation. 

This project will be developed using the open-source Godot engine. The original repo and character controller was written in C# for .NET Godot; however, I’m going to transfer this over to GDScript and continue from there. Why? It’s first class in all docs / official tutorials, I'm not doing anything seriously performance heavy, and I don't really mind learning a new language.  

Yes, this is a lofty goal, but it's not impossible. Some of the most popular games in history have been developed by individuals or extremely small teams, Stardew Valley and Hollow knight being among them. I seriously doubt this project will achieve the same success, but I’m not just going to sit here, mindlessly applying for jobs as time disappears.      

Thanks for reading this dev log (rant?). Future posts will focus more on the project. For now though I need to download godot, build a website and start translating C# in GDScript.
